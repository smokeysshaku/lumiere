# Tlhago Agri Pro — analysis of the business profile, and what changed in the rewrite

Source: `Business_profile_22.docx` (Tlhago Agri Pro (Pty) Ltd, Reg No 2025/973670/07)
Rewrite: `Tlhago_Agri_Pro_Funding_Proposal.docx`

---

## 1. What the original document is

A 21-section business profile for a community agriculture project at Ga-Matankane
Village, Lepelle-Nkumpi Municipality, Capricorn District, Limpopo. It covers health
and education status, objectives, services, management structure, marketing,
industrial analysis, market research, expansion, production cycle, SWOT, and a
budget table of R332 700 on the final page.

It reads as a **business-plan template filled in**, not as a pitch. A funder
skims for four things — what you do, why you, what you need, what comes back —
and in the original all four are scattered across pages 1, 8, and 14.

---

## 2. The seven problems, in order of how much they cost you

### 2.1 It cannot decide whether it is a charity or a business
Paragraph one: *"established as a philanthropic project."* But the entity is a
(Pty) Ltd with markets, agents, retailers, revenue and a capital budget. Grant
funders read "Pty Ltd" and stop. Investors and lenders read "philanthropic" and
stop. Nobody finishes the document knowing which pile to put it in.

**Fixed by:** leading with a commercial farm that employs its community and pays
surplus into a trust. The social outcome is the *result*, not the business model.
That version qualifies for enterprise-development funding, agri grants, blended
finance, and CSI money all at once.

### 2.2 The ask is on the last page, and it is unexplained
R332 700 appears in a table headed *"24. bugdet breakdown for project 1"* after
21 sections of context, with no sentence saying what it is for or what it produces.

**Fixed by:** the amount is the first thing on page 1, in a box, with the
one-sentence reason next to it.

### 2.3 The land figures contradict each other three times
| Where | Figure |
|---|---|
| Section 1 | "goal to secure a **446 hectare** field" |
| Section 14 | "a plot that covers **5 hectares**… a portion that can cover over **50 hectares**" |
| Budget | fertiliser "on **4,5 hectors**" |

A funder spots this in thirty seconds, and it makes every other number suspect.

**Fixed by:** the proposal is for **5 hectares**, full stop, with ~50 more named
as Phase 2 and further extension as Phase 3. One number per phase.

### 2.4 "Our village has a population of less than 50"
Read literally, you are asking R332 700 to serve fewer than fifty people, while
the surrounding paragraph cites 1.4 million district residents to make the case
look bigger. The contrast makes the big number look like padding.

**This is almost certainly a data error** — 50 *households*, or 500, or 5 000
people. It needs your real figure. Until then the rewrite anchors the problem on
the village and the municipality without repeating the "<50" claim.

### 2.5 There is no revenue anywhere in the document
Not a yield, not a price, not a tonne, not a timeline, not a break-even. The
budget shows what money is *spent on* and never what it *produces*. For a funder
that is the single most important missing page — see §4 below.

### 2.6 The scope is a dozen businesses at once
Crops + tomato sauce + mango atchar + butternut packing + goats + sheep + wool
yarn + piggery + a community trust + roads + parks + libraries + a primary school
+ a skills school + health facilities. Each one is credible on its own. Together
they read as unfocused, and they make the R332 700 look like a down-payment on
something enormous.

**Fixed by:** one funded thing (red onions on 5 ha) and everything else clearly
labelled Phase 2 and Phase 3 — *"We are asking you to fund Phase 1. The rest is
what Phase 1 makes possible."* Ambition kept, risk visibly contained.

### 2.7 The risk section reads as denial
> *"We only have one weakness which is lack of equipment."*
> *"We are only threatened by the natural disaster."*

No experienced funder believes a business with one weakness. Worse, the document
elsewhere names a much bigger risk and never treats it as one: **the land is held
by the Chief and the Induna, and there is no signed lease.** No lender finances a
R250 000 tractor onto land the applicant has no written right to use. Stating the
risk *and* the mitigation is what buys trust.

**Fixed by:** a five-row risk table — land tenure, the missing river crossing,
price-taking at the fresh produce market, weather, and not owning equipment yet —
each with what you actually do about it.

## 3. Smaller things that cost credibility

- **Numbering is broken throughout.** Auto-numbered headings mixed with manual
  prefixes — `13.`, `14.1`, `21,3,`, `20.1.1` — and lists that restart at i)
  mid-section. Several sections run together in one paragraph (`21.SWOT ANALYSIS 21.1.STRENGTH`).
- **Typos in headings and totals:** `OPHANS` → ORPHANS, `VULNARABLE` → VULNERABLE,
  `bugdet` → budget, **`GRANT TOTAL` → GRAND TOTAL** (this one sits under the money),
  `hectors` → hectares, `tomatoes source` → tomato sauce, `archer` → atchar,
  `ranting` → renting, `gowth` → growth, `Vilage` → Village.
- **The header repeats the company name four times** on every page.
- **Document metadata author is "FETAKGOMO SECHOOL"** — a reused school template.
  Anyone who opens File → Properties sees it.
- **Nine consecutive empty paragraphs** between the education and objectives
  sections, and blank page breaks elsewhere.
- **"NOTE: Project plan is on the separate page attached below"** — nothing is attached.
- **No named team.** No project manager, no board members, no advisors, by name.
- **The best line in the document is on page 8:** *"We exist so that people can
  eat, it is as simple as that."* That is your opening sentence. It is now the
  first line of the "Why we exist" section.

## 4. What the rewrite does with your budget

Your arithmetic is correct — the nine line items do sum to R332 700. But grouped
flat, the tractor swamps everything and the ask looks like R332 700 of *cost*.
Regrouped, it tells a much stronger story:

| Group | Amount | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Equipment (once-off) | R280 250 | Assets you still own in ten years |
| Growing costs (one season) | R42 450 | Repeats each season |
| People | R10 000 | 50 workers × R200, straight into local households |
| **Grand total** | **R332 700** | |

**84% of the ask is once-off capital.** A season after this one costs **R52 450**
to run. That single fact — same numbers, regrouped — is the most persuasive thing
in your budget and the original never says it.

## 5. What you must fill in before sending

Three highlighted blanks in the new document, plus two corrections:

1. **Season-one revenue** (highlighted, "What comes back" section). Onion revenue
   is yield × price. Get three numbers from your market agent: expected tonnes per
   hectare, the price per 10 kg bag at Tshwane Market, and therefore the season
   total. **Do not send the proposal without this.** A funder needs to see the
   R332 700 come back.
2. **Project manager bio** (highlighted) — one line on your farming background
   and years of experience.
3. **Date** on the cover line (highlighted).
4. **The village population figure** — confirm the real number before you use it
   anywhere. "Less than 50" was left out of the rewrite for this reason.
5. **The 13% tertiary-qualification figure** — cite where it comes from (StatsSA
   Census, municipal IDP) or soften it to "very few". Uncited statistics invite
   the wrong kind of question.

## 6. Two things to do before the proposal goes out

- **Get the land lease in writing** and signed by the traditional authority.
  It is the first item under Objectives for a reason, and it is referenced in the
  risk table. Attaching a signed lease will do more for this application than
  another page of prose.
- **Attach evidence.** Company registration certificate, site photographs, a
  letter of intent from one market agent. The original claims "multiple agents
  who are willing to sell our products at City Deep, Tshwane fresh produce" —
  one letter turns that claim into a fact.

## 7. Structure: before and after

| Original (21 sections, ~2 250 words) | Rewrite (10 sections, ~1 100 words) |
|---|---|
| 1 Business profile / 2 Health / 3 Education | The problem |
| 4 Objectives | Our objectives (12 months / 3–5 years) |
| 5–8 Activities, nutrition, orphans, trust | What we do · What comes back |
| 9–11 Management, coordinator, PM, board | Who we are |
| 12 Why we exist *(page 8)* | **Why we exist** *(page 1)* |
| 12–13 Industrial analysis, market research | Why it works here |
| 14 Expansion, agro-processing, goats, piggery | Where this goes (Phase 2 / Phase 3) |
| 15 Market risk / 21 SWOT | The risks, honestly |
| 16–20 Production cycle, retailers, evaluation, resources | *(cut — operational detail, not pitch material)* |
| 24 Budget *(last page)* | **The ask** *(page 1)* + What the money buys |

Nothing true was thrown away. The operational detail that was cut — production
cycles, infrastructure lists, quality-control procedures, resource schedules —
belongs in the **full business plan you send when a funder asks for one**. Keep
the original document for exactly that purpose.
